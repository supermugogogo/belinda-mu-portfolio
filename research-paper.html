Capstone Research Paper

Teachers' Global Perspectives on Restorative Approaches to Classroom Management

A praxis capstone proposal and literature review by Zongchao (Belinda) Mu.

Research overview

This praxis capstone examines restorative approaches to classroom management as alternatives to punitive discipline. It focuses on how community-building, accountability, reflection, and relationship repair can support positive K-12 learning environments across cultural contexts.

Context and Motivation

Restorative school practices, also described as positive discipline, responsive classroom, or empowerment, aim to build a sense of community, prevent conflict, and respond to misconduct by helping students accept responsibility and rebuild relationships. The project grew from Belinda's experience as an international teacher in the United States and from a desire to understand how restorative principles translate across cultural expectations of classroom management.

Research Focus

The guiding topic is Teachers' Global Perspectives on Restorative Approaches to Classroom Management. The project uses a podcast series to invite K-12 educators with experience in the United States, China, and Chile to discuss their approaches, challenges, and practical insights.

Participants and Dissemination

Participants include educators working in high school, kindergarten immersion, and elementary Mandarin immersion contexts. Each participant contributes a recorded conversation on restorative practice and classroom management. After the series, findings and takeaways are shared with a school faculty group to encourage reflection and discussion.

Equity and Learning Outcomes

The capstone promotes cooperative learning, constructive conflict, student responsibility, and problem solving. By foregrounding educators with international experience, it adds culturally responsive perspectives to conversations about restorative practice and supports professional learning around diversity, equity, inclusion, and supportive classroom communities.

  • Learning Outcome 2: Define features of a supportive learning environment through international perspectives on community, responsibility, and relationship repair.
  • Learning Outcome 3: Collaborate with colleagues and administrators to consider equitable approaches to classroom management and school-wide implementation.

Theoretical Framework

The study draws on research about cooperative contexts, constructive conflict, civic values, empathetic relationships, and culturally situated classroom management. Restorative practice is approached as a long-term community-building process rather than a quick disciplinary intervention. Reflection, open dialogue, shared agreements, and meaningful student voice are treated as central conditions for repairing harm and sustaining trust.

Methodology and Analysis

Participants receive guiding questions before each podcast conversation and are invited to suggest relevant examples or additional topics. Individual episodes are recorded, transcribed verbatim, and analyzed through thematic analysis. First-cycle coding organizes the transcripts around restorative-practice concepts and participants' contexts; second-cycle coding compares, condenses, and refines these categories into themes.

Literature Review

Restorative justice in the classroom has gained attention as an alternative approach to discipline and conflict resolution, emphasizing the importance of cooperative contexts, constructive conflict, and civic values. This literature review illuminates seven important studies that shed light on the significance and implications of restorative justice in educational settings.

Johnson and Johnson (2012) explore the necessary roles of cooperative context, constructive conflict, and civic values in implementing restorative justice in the classroom. They argue that creating a cooperative environment where students work collaboratively with mutual respect is crucial. Constructive conflict, viewed as an opportunity for learning and growth, encourages reflection, open dialogue, and problem-solving. Moreover, fostering civic values promotes responsibility, empathy, and fairness among students, facilitating a restorative community where students take accountability for their actions and work towards repairing harm. The article takes civic virtues in community as an example to support the significance of civic values in the context of restorative justice initiatives in educational environments. Civic values reflect the behaviors that are important for the success of the community. They are inherent in the values that members of a community live by and are essential for the establishment of restorative justice. For a community to exist and be sustained, its members must share common goals and values aimed at improving the quality of life within that community.

Similarly, Choi, Johnson, and Johnson (2011) investigated the importance of creating cooperative environments where students work collaboratively with mutual respect. They examined the relationships between cooperative learning experiences, social interdependence, aggression, victimization, and prosocial behaviors, concluding that a predisposition for cooperation predicts prosocial behaviors and the absence of harm-intended aggression, while a competitive predisposition predicts harm-intended aggression. Cooperative experiences contribute to the development of cooperation as a personality trait. The study measured cooperative predisposition and competitive predisposition in elementary school children using social interdependence scales. These scales assessed students' individual tendencies towards cooperative, competitive, and individualistic behaviors. These results have significant implications for preventing bullying and promoting prosocial behaviors.

Homrich-Knieling (2019) also explored aspects associated with the cooperative learning community. The study discusses the shift from classroom management to community-building practices. The article suggests strategies for building empathetic relationships, such as daily check-ins, community agreements, and meetings that delegate power to students. It also warns against practices that may hinder relationship-building, including publicly highlighting minor negative behaviors, using data walls with individual markers, restricting students from conversing in other languages, and negative emotional interactions with students.

Evans and Lester (2013) discuss the principles and challenges of implementing restorative justice in education. They propose principles such as meeting needs, providing accountability and support, viewing conflict as a learning opportunity, building healthy learning communities, restoring relationships, and addressing power imbalances. However, they also highlight various challenges, including time constraints, funding limitations, staff training, and the need for clarity in defining and applying restorative justice principles.

Those challenges and barriers are further supported by Johnson's (2020) study. The research suggests that incorporating a restorative practice framework may not have an immediate effect on teachers' perceptions of students' conflict resolution abilities, self-efficacy in managing classroom discipline, classroom climate as perceived by students, or the decrease in discipline referrals. The article emphasizes that the implementation of restorative justice in a school community is a long-term process that requires consistent leadership, support, and collaboration from all stakeholders.

Further, Graham (2018) and Wubbels (2011) highlight the relationship between teachers and restorative justice practice in the classroom. Graham (2018) explores the integration of authority and democracy in classroom management, highlighting their complementary nature. The author proposes a framework that acknowledges both teacher authority and student agency. Without teacher authority, the classroom can become chaotic, hindering academic guidance and increasing vulnerability to bullying. Without a democratic learning environment, oppressive power dynamics may prevail, neglecting students' needs and equity. The article emphasizes the importance of balancing teacher power and responsibility while providing opportunities for student voice and decision-making.

Wubbels (2011) focuses on classroom management from an international perspective, highlighting the challenges faced by teachers worldwide. It discusses the shift from behavioral to internal approaches and acknowledges cultural differences in classroom management practices. The importance of understanding local circumstances and individual variations within cultures is emphasized. Strategies such as careful observation, rewards, punishments, modeling, and building relationships are recommended. Therefore, the article emphasizes the significance of effective classroom management for teacher satisfaction and the need for prospective teachers to develop necessary skills in this area.

This literature review provides valuable insights into the significance and implications of restorative justice in the classroom, emphasizing its potential to create cooperative, supportive learning environments, prevent bullying, and promote positive social interactions among students. It underscores the importance of considering cultural variations, understanding local circumstances, and fostering effective classroom management practices to enhance the implementation of restorative justice initiatives in educational settings.
